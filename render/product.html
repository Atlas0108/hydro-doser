Hydro Doser

A nutrient doser for small hydroponic gardens

It holds a tank of water and the two nutrient bottles, and on a schedule it pumps water and the right amount of each nutrient into the garden's reservoir. There are three pumps inside and one bundle of tubes coming out of the back.

Scroll
01 · The tank

The water tank

The tank is a 5.7 litre Cambro food container, which is dishwasher-safe and costs a few dollars to replace. You lift it off to fill it. The pickup tube goes in through a hole drilled in the lid and rests on the bottom.

02 · The bottles

The nutrient bottles

The two pockets hold a 125 mL bottle in a printed sleeve, or a 1 L bottle without one. There is a slot in the front so you can see how much is left. Each nutrient has its own pump and its own tube all the way to the reservoir, because two-part concentrates form a precipitate if they meet before they are diluted.

03 · The column

The column

The tubes run through three channels in the column and down into the base, so none of them are loose. The three outlet tubes and the float switch lead leave the back of the unit in one sleeve and clip onto the rim of the reservoir, above the water line, so a line can't siphon on its own.

04 · The pumps

The pumps

There are three peristaltic pumps: one for water and one for each nutrient. They sit behind covers on the front of the base. The only part of a peristaltic pump that wears out is its tube, and you can change it by taking the cover off, without moving the tank or opening the rest of the unit.

05 · The base

The base

The base is two printed trays bolted together. The ESP32 and the pump driver boards are in the tray under the column, so they are not under the tank if it ever leaks. There are two buttons and a status light on the front, and the power jack and the tube bundle are on the back.

What it does

You tell it how many millilitres of each nutrient to add per litre of water. After that, topping up the reservoir and feeding it happen in the same run.

Most hydroponic gardens want a bit of concentrate added each time you top up the reservoir, so that's what the doser does: it pumps in the water, then nutrient A, then nutrient B, each through its own tube. A float switch sitting in the reservoir stops any run when the water reaches it, and if the float isn't plugged in nothing will run at all. The doser keeps a log of what it has pumped and counts down how much is left in the tank until you refill it.

Specification
TankCambro CamSquare 6 qt (5.7 L), translucent polypropylene, NSF listed, with volume markings. It lifts off to fill.
BottlesTwo pockets that take bottles up to 92 mm across: 125 mL bottles in a sleeve, or 500 mL and 1 L bottles as they are.
PumpsThree 12 V peristaltic pumps on 3 × 5 mm food-grade silicone tube, 19 to 100 mL/min, each calibrated separately.
DosingTops up with water and adds nutrients in proportion to the water added. Stops when the float switch trips. Every run is logged.
ControlsA Dose button, a Stop button and a status light on the front. Home Assistant over WiFi through ESPHome. A USB port for updates.
PowerA 12 V, 2 A plug-in adapter. Only one pump runs at a time, and the input is fused.
Size345 × 225 × 315 mm. About 2 kg empty and 8 kg with a full tank.
ConnectionsOne sleeved bundle to the garden, carrying three tubes and the float switch lead, ending in a clip that hangs on the reservoir's rim.
