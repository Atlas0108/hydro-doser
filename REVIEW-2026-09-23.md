# Rise Doser design review, 2026-09-23

An independent review of the design as of this date (model, README, verify
harness, firmware), by a reviewer with no part in the design. Kept here
because the next round of changes is driven by it. Nothing in it has been
acted on yet.

Files reviewed: `rise-doser.scad`, `README.md`, `verify.sh`, `export.sh`,
`stl/*`, `firmware/rise-doser/{README.md,rise-doser.yaml,doser.h}`. The
reviewer rendered the assembly and each piece, ran STL volume, bed-contact
and overhang statistics, and ran extra boolean probes in the style of
`verify.sh`.

Note: the firmware's pump timeout is 45 min (`doser.h`), not 30.

## 1. Mechanical

**Blocker: the column as exported stands on its two rails, not its floor.**
The export shifts by `-tray_h + 3`, so the rails (2.5 × 176 × 3 mm) touch
the bed and the entire 120 × 200 floor is 3 mm in the air. From
`stl/column.stl`: bed contact 714 mm²; downward-facing floor at z = 3 mm:
22,594 mm². `verify.sh` passes because it only checks lowest z = 0. It
cannot print without supports. Fix: delete the rails; locate the column with
the tray's four posts extended 3 mm above the wall and matching blind
recesses in the column floor.

**Should fix: rails are dimensionally wrong.** 0.3 mm clearance to a 114 mm
printed cavity is inside PETG shrink (~0.4 mm). Interfere or rattle.

**Should fix: the column's screw heads stand proud on the pocket floor.**
The counterbore starts at z = 69.99, above the pocket floor (z = 70), so it
cuts nothing. Four pan heads sit on the floor; the sleeve rocks on them. The
screws are also 3.6–4 mm from the pocket wall: a driver can't reach them
down a 94 mm bore 216 mm deep. Move them ≥ 20 mm inboard, countersink.

**Should fix: dock plates are 4 mm PETG spanning ~190 mm under 4 kg with
nothing under them.** Material between bulkhead top (z = 50) and plate
underside (z = 62): 0. Deflection ~1 mm solid, 2–3 mm at 15 % infill, and
PETG creeps: expect a permanent dish. Raise `bulk_h` to `tray_h - 0.5` so
the bulkhead carries the plate mid-span; add ribs under the plate.

**Should fix: the curb is 0.9 mm tall.** Bead centred at z = 69.5, plate top
at 70. Won't locate a Cambro with a real foot ring; the 6 mm-offset check
passes only because the bin is modelled sharp-cornered and flat. Make it
3–4 mm, or a recessed pocket.

**Should fix: heat-set posts are 8 × 8 with a 4 mm hole: 2 mm walls.**
Inserting will bulge or split them. 10 × 10, or gusset to the wall.

**Consider: seam joint.** Two M3 at z = 30 only; nothing ties the tops of
adjacent trays, so the 512 mm base can hinge about the bolt line when lifted
with 8 kg of water in it. Add a bolt near the top, or make the plates/column
overlap the seam. Bolt heads and nuts aren't modelled; the front one sits
13 mm from a pump's tube.

**Consider: bulkhead.** After the U-slot and the tube window, each pump hangs
on two M3 self-tappers in a 14.5 mm strip of 3 mm wall. Adequate for 150 g,
but see access. The comment "diagonal pair used" is stale; both screws are
the lower pair.

**Consider: the centre tray has two 9 × 36 mm slots through its floor to
the outside.** The column's slot is at z = 62..71, the tray's at z = -1..5;
no tube uses them. They're open holes in the underside, and `verify.sh`
line 63 requires them. Delete, or call them drains and add matching ones
under the docks.

## 2. Printability

**Blocker: column orientation** (above).

**Should fix: the column's top slab bridges 6,634 mm² of void**, longest span
~57 mm at the waist, in PETG. The tube holes and side exits land in that
region: stringy underside, ragged holes. Add ribs, roof the voids with 45°
chamfers, or make the column a figure-8 (two tangent cylinders plus a rear
chase) — which also halves its mass.

**Should fix: mass.** Column 869 cm³ (~1.0 kg as printed, all 3 mm walls);
each sleeve 1,094 cm³ (a solid 93 mm cylinder, ~340 g at 15 %); dock trays
333 cm³; centre tray 239; plates 158 each. ≈ 2.8 kg PETG and ~100 h. The
column and sleeves are ~60 % of it. The sleeve should be a thin cup on
fins (~40 g).

**Consider:** bottom fillet r 2.5 starts at a horizontal tangent (60–80°
overhang for the first 0.5 mm): use a 45° chamfer or r ≤ 1.5. Seam tube
holes and button holes are plain circles (the teardrop helper exists,
unused). Sleeve-to-pocket fit is 0.5 mm radial: press fit after shrink; use
≥ 1 mm. Everything needs a brim; the README doesn't say.

## 3. Plumbing

**Blocker: the tee mixes Sprout and Thrive concentrate undiluted.** The
firmware README promises they never touch undiluted; the tee does exactly
that in ~470 mm of shared line (3–4 mL). Two-part concentrates are kept
apart because calcium in one precipitates with phosphate/sulfate in the
other; that precipitate forms in the shared line and clogs it. The dead
volume is also about one 125 mL-bottle dose, so each dose arrives one run
late, and roller leakage pushes one concentrate toward the other bottle.
Give each nutrient its own line and lid hole (4 exits on the right, 4 holes
in the mix lid).

**Blocker: submerged outlets create siphons the float can't stop.** Levels
up to z ≈ 247, tube peak 272, pumps at 34, a 1 L bottle's liquid up to ~280.
Once primed, a full tube siphons regardless of the peak; the only thing
holding it back is roller occlusion in an $8 pump whose tube flattens with
age. So: the water tank drains into the mix tank until levels equalise; a
full 1 L bottle siphons concentrate on its own; the transfer line siphons
mix → Rise or Rise → mix depending on which surface is higher, unseen by
float or firmware. "Tubes sit on the bottom" must apply to pickups only.
Every inlet (water and nutrients into the mix tank, transfer into the Rise)
must end above the maximum liquid level so the down-leg drains. This also
improves dose accuracy.

**Should fix:** mix-tank inlets sit 14 mm from the transfer pickup on the
floor; concentrate pools unstirred and the transfer takes a concentrated
slug first. Inlets above the surface, pickup at the far corner, ideally an
air stone.

**Should fix: barb-to-window path forces a ~9 mm bend radius** (barbs at
±29, window at ±38, barb y = 19, bulkhead y = 40). 1/4" silicone kinks below
~15–20 mm. The harness tests two straight stubs, not the curve. Move the
window outboard 20–25 mm or the bulkhead back; add a swept-path check.

**Should fix: exit-to-lid geometry.** Exit centre 272, lid top 263.3: 8.7 mm
of drop over 30 mm; tubes will loop up on the lids. The assert margin is
1.7 mm on estimated lid dimensions; a real SFC2 lid is likely thicker than
6 mm. There's 33 mm of bed headroom: raise `col_h` ~25 mm or angle the
exits 45° down.

**Consider:** no prime step in the Dose workflow (~6 mL of concentrate to
fill a line). Backflow water ↔ mix through the water line (solved by the
air gap). No flush routine; nutrient line tips crust, the water line grows
biofilm. Lifting a tank: the bin rises ~190 mm before the pickups clear,
then drips onto the plate over the electronics. README still says "bottom
valve", "valve pin", "2 × M3 per valve", "pump heads come through the
front", "motor holes are teardrops", "tube groove": none of it in the model.

## 4. Usability

**Should fix: the panel has no firmware.** No `binary_sensor` for the two
buttons, no output for the light, no LED in the BOM. "Dose" is ambiguous.

**Should fix: the electronics have no standoffs.** Boards float 6 mm up on
nothing; the DevKit's headers go 6 mm down into air.

**Should fix: pump flange screws are unreachable.** Both at z = 13, axis
along y, behind the bulkhead, 9 mm above the floor and 7 mm below the motor,
in a bay open only at the top. And the pump's cover (where the wear tube
lives) faces a closed wall 2 mm away: every tube change means lifting a
4 kg tank and its tubes, the plate off, the pump unscrewed from behind.
Peristaltic tubes wear in months. Use the standard mount: head through a
removable front bezel, screws from the front, behind a snap cover if it
must be hidden.

**Should fix: no wire route out of the left tray.** The Water and Sprout
leads must share the two 14 mm seam holes with two 6.35 mm tubes each. Add
a wire hole per seam and a cable path for the panel and jack.

**Consider:** a 3.8 L tank is two 2 L batches; still weekly refills. A fill
plug in the water lid avoids moving tubes. Dry tanks aren't detected: a dry
water run followed by a nutrient dose leaves concentrate alone on the mix
floor with the counter at 2 L. A float per tank fixes most of §5 too.
Buttons at 20 mm pitch with 15.5 mm bezels; the USB face is 6 mm inside the
wall, most micro-USB plugs won't seat through 3 mm + 3 mm of air.

## 5. Safety and reliability

**Should fix: electronics sit directly under the mix tank.** No lip or
gasket at the plate; spills, tube drips, condensation and any overfill (the
mix counter is dead-reckoned; `mix_capacity` defaults to 4.0 L for a 3.8 L
bin) run over the plate edge into the tray with the 12 V rail. Move the
electronics to the centre tray or a sealed rear bay, give the plates a drip
lip/drain, default `mix_capacity` ~3.0 L, and add the wet sensor before
first use.

**Should fix: Rise overflow.** The float is fail-safe open, but not for a
float stuck "not full" (fouled by roots or algae, common). The other cap is
`min(want, mix_ml)`, so the worst case is 3.8 L into a full reservoir.
`transfer_amount` defaults to 0 = all. Default ~1 L, add a rolling 24 h cap.

**Consider:** 45 min at 100 mL/min = 4.5 L, more than either tank; the
timeout protects nothing tank-sized. Algae in translucent bins under grow
light, with mix sitting indefinitely: opaque bins or a shroud, or
mix-and-transfer as one action. Stalled INTLLAB pumps draw ~1 A; two
paralleled ULN2003 channels are rated 1 A, no fuse, no current sense: add a
polyfuse, consider MOSFETs. The 5 A adapter is 2× what's needed.

## 6. Product viability

- Bought parts ≈ $95, plus ~2.8 kg PETG (~$60) and ~100 h of print time. Not
  sellable as printed; the print time alone is the objection.
- A manufacturer would object to: seven bolted prints with inserts (→ two
  moulded modules, bosses and snap fits); drilled third-party lids (→ custom
  lid with grommets and a fill plug); a DevKit and stepper-kit drivers with
  an exposed USB (→ one PCB: ESP32 module, MOSFETs, fuse, jack, buttons,
  LED); pumps that aren't user-serviceable (→ front-loading cassettes); no
  level sensing; electronics under the wet zone; translucent tanks; a float
  installed in another company's reservoir (liability; Rise geometry varies
  by model).
- **The mix tank is the biggest cost driver:** it adds a tank, a pump, two
  lid holes, the tee, half the plumbing and most of the siphon and
  stratification risk. The Rise reservoir is ~19 L of circulating water and
  the Rise app already has you squirt concentrate straight into it. Dosing
  into the Rise directly with a water top-off is a 3-pump, 1-tank product at
  about half the parts. Worth an explicit decision.
- For a moulded version the layout survives, but: 2–2.5 mm walls with ribs,
  drafts on the pockets, no through-slots in floors, moulded cable and tube
  channels, a sealed electronics bay, a bigger water tank.

## 7. What `verify.sh` doesn't check

1. Bed-contact area per piece (would have caught the column).
2. Unsupported downward-facing area above z = 0.
3. Tube continuity as a swept path with a bend-radius limit, not disjoint
   straight stubs.
4. Bolt heads and nuts vs tubes and pumps.
5. Wire routes and a cable cross-section budget through the seams.
6. Plate support mid-span.
7. Curb height as a number.
8. Board standoffs (the boards check passes *because* nothing supports them).
9. Tool access: a driver-shaped cylinder to each column screw and each
   flange screw.
10. Lid lift (bins only; lids clear by 3 mm).
11. Volume, mass and print time per piece.
12. Sensitivity to the estimated bought-part dimensions (the exit-height
    assert has 1.7 mm of margin).
13. It enforces a mistake: line 63 requires a through-hole in the centre
    tray floor.

Stale in the README: "print the centre tray and one sleeve first, they test
the pump holes and flange screws." The centre tray has no pumps.

## Top 10, ranked

1. Re-orient the column so it prints on its floor: delete the rails, locate
   on the tray posts (extended 3 mm) with recesses in the column floor.
2. Remove the tee; separate lines and lid holes for Sprout and Thrive.
3. End every inlet above the liquid line; only pickups sit on the bottom.
4. Get the electronics out from under the mix tank; drip lip and drain on
   the plates; wet sensor and a mix-tank float before first use.
5. Make the pumps serviceable: head through a removable front bezel, screws
   from the front, snap cover if it must be hidden.
6. Cut the plastic: figure-8 column, thin-cup sleeve; target < 1.5 kg.
7. Support the dock plates: bulkhead to the plate underside, ribs, and a real
   3–4 mm curb.
8. Fix the column screws: real counterbores, ≥ 20 mm from the pocket wall.
9. Standoffs, wire holes, and panel firmware.
10. Bound the failure modes in firmware: `mix_capacity` ≤ 3.0 L for a 3.8 L
    bin, `transfer_amount` ~1 L with a daily cap, the 45 min timeout as a
    backstop only. Then decide deliberately whether the mix tank should
    exist at all.
